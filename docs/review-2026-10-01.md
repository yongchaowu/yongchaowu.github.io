# Full review — 2026-10-01

Scope: this repository (`yongchaowu.github.io`) as a Jekyll/GitHub Pages site, as a
content system derived from cnblogs, and as a public repository. Every number below was
measured on 2026-10-01 against a clean local build, not estimated.

Companion documents: `docs/technical-review-2026-09-25.md` (factual cross-checks of
article claims) and `AGENTS.md` (operational rules). This document is the engineering,
content-supply-chain and risk review; it does not re-check article facts.

---

## 1. Goal

The project is a two-stage migration, not a blog written from scratch:

1. Articles are published first on cnblogs — <https://www.cnblogs.com/yongchao> — which
   remains the **source of truth** for the articles. The upstream working directory is
   `~/Workspace/Blog/`, and the authoritative document for that side is
   `~/Workspace/Blog/AGENTS.md`. The latest backup is
   `~/Workspace/Blog/backup/cnblogs_blog_yongchao.20261001000949.zip` (1.2 MB); the
   extracted 2026-10-01 mirror held **346 articles**.
2. This repository is the **derived publication target**, whose job is to correct,
   refine, organize and synthesize that material into a self-owned site at
   <https://blog.wyclswq.top>.

So the design goal is not "a blog". It is: *preserve an import faithfully, record every
divergence from the source, and layer an editorial reading experience on top — without
ever losing the ability to audit what changed and why.*

## 2. Review perspectives used

Four passes, each asking a different question. They are recorded separately because they
produce genuinely different findings, and because pass 4 exists mainly to record the
mistakes passes 1–3 made.

**Pass 1 — Engineering surface.** Treat the repository as a Jekyll site: templates, data
files, build, CI, SEO output, payload. Question: *is the generated site correct and cheap?*
Produced findings 1–3, 5–11 below.

**Pass 2 — Content supply chain.** Stop treating the repository as the artifact. Question:
*who actually writes these articles, and what is being preserved?* Fetched the live
cnblogs blog, read `~/Workspace/Blog/AGENTS.md`, compared the export against `_posts/`.
Produced findings 4, 14, and corrections C3–C6.

**Pass 3 — Adversarial.** Three hostile questions: *what happens to a stranger arriving
from search?*, *what is in the public git history that should not be?*, *what does this
cost the maintainer in two years?* Produced findings 4 (privacy half), 12, 13, and the
reader-journey argument.

**Pass 4 — Reconciliation.** Fold in the confirmed upstream model, re-verify every number
that passes 1–3 asserted, and downgrade or retract what did not survive. This pass removed
one P0 and corrected five substantive claims. Corrections are in §5.

## 3. What is genuinely strong

Worth stating plainly, because it constrains which recommendations are worth making.

- **The divergence ledger is real and well designed.** `_data/format_fixes.yml` binds each
  approved correction to an exact resulting blob with a stated reason, and
  `verify-post-history.rb` enforces a 331-post baseline against it. Under the fork model
  this is precisely the right instrument: it makes "we changed this away from the cnblogs
  original, here is why" auditable rather than invisible. (See C3 — I initially
  misjudged this.)
- **External-link and injection hygiene are clean.** All 2,069 `target="_blank"` anchors
  carry `rel="noopener"`. Every `innerHTML` write in the shipped JS passes through `esc()`.
- **The search index is a genuinely good piece of engineering.** Two-phase load, phase 1 at
  ~46 KB gzip so the first keystroke never waits, phase 2 deferred via
  `requestIdleCallback` with a `sessionStorage` cache, and the source comment records the
  before/after measurement (2.5 MB / 57 s → split). The phase-2 payload is too large and
  too eagerly fetched (finding 7), but the design thinking is sound.
- **Tag pages are server-rendered.** All 274 carry real post links inside `<main>`; only
  the `/tag/` index needs `posts-meta.json`. (See C2 — I initially got this wrong.)
- **`baseurl` discipline is complete.** Internal links go through `relative_url`, and
  `smoke-test.rb` genuinely asserts the Pages base path.
- **Editorial metadata is unusually rigorous** for a personal blog: 53 sidecar records, 122
  claim-level citation links, and visible `AI-assisted` / `Imported` / `Review required` /
  `Version-sensitive` / `not verified` states.

## 4. Findings

Severity is about consequence, not effort.

### P0

**F1 — RSS: 7 of the 10 newest items have an empty description.**
`feed.xml` renders `post.excerpt` and ignores `post.summary`. The seven newest posts put
`<!--more-->` on the first line of the body, so `excerpt == "\n"`. All seven have a
hand-written `summary:` that the homepage and archive use correctly.
```
1. desc=  1  LLM 端点连不上？把"不通"拆成四层来查…   EMPTY
…
7. desc=  1  清空 Linux 终端代理环境变量…                EMPTY
8. desc=577  GPU / CUDA / Docker 部署兼容性清单
```
Fix is one line: `{{ post.summary | default: post.excerpt | strip_html | normalize_whitespace | truncate: 300 | xml_escape }}`.
The feed also lacks `<language>`, `<managingEditor>` and `<ttl>`, carries only 10 items,
and sets `<lastBuildDate>{{ site.time }}`, which changes on every deploy and makes readers
re-fetch every item as "updated".

**F2 — `vendor/` is committed: 3,179 files, 56 MB working tree, 46 MB of git objects.**
`_config.yml` excludes it from the Pages artifact but nothing excludes it from git, and
`.gitignore` does not mention it. `.bundle/config` hard-codes `BUNDLE_PATH: "vendor/bundle"`,
so any local `bundle install` recreates it in place and the next `git add -A` re-commits
it. The largest history blobs are native binaries: 5.0 MB `sass.snapshot`, 4.5 MB `dart`,
1.2 MB `rubyeventmachine.so`, 893 KB `ffi_c.so`, plus `.gem` caches. The vendored tree is
Ruby **3.0.0** while CI runs **3.2**, so it is not even usable as an offline fallback — it
is pure dead weight, and every public clone pays for it.
Fix: `git rm -r --cached vendor/`, add `vendor/` and `.bundle/` to `.gitignore`. History
size is a separate, optional `git filter-repo` decision.

**F3 — Language metadata is wrong for 267 of 357 articles.**
`site.lang: en`; only 71 posts declare `lang` (14 `en`, 57 `zh-CN`). 267 posts contain CJK
text and declare nothing, so they inherit `en` and emit `<html lang="en">`,
`og:locale="en"` and JSON-LD `"inLanguage": "en"`. Because historical post bodies may not
be rewritten, the fix belongs in a sidecar (`_data/post_langs.yml`) consulted by
`_includes/head.html` and `_includes/seo-jsonld.html`, not in front-matter edits.

**F4 — Unpublished personal material is in public git history.**
`_drafts/` is fully tracked, so its contents ship in every clone, are indexed by GitHub
code search, and are archivable by third parties. Git has no notion of "private"; Jekyll
simply never builds `_drafts/`, which is not the same thing as being private. The directory
holds four private-diary entries with hour-by-hour routine logging and personal reflection
(including a real QQ ID), plus a team-building retrospective with private anecdotes.
This is the one finding whose remedy gets *harder* over time, and the only one where the
fix cannot recall what already left. Removing it requires rewriting history.

### P1

**F5 — Duplicate and incomplete structured data on all 357 posts.**
`_layouts/post.html` emits `<article itemscope itemtype="http://schema.org/BlogPosting">`
containing no `itemprop` at all, while `_includes/seo-jsonld.html` independently emits a
complete `["TechArticle","BlogPosting"]` graph. Two competing graphs per URL, one of them
a field-less Article, and a non-`https` schema.org IRI. Delete the two attributes; the
JSON-LD already covers the page.

**F6 — 68% of tags serve exactly one post.**
189 of 277 tag names are used once, 32 twice, 12 three times. `tag_aliases.yml` holds 7
aliases. The tag pages are properly rendered, so this is a content-strategy problem rather
than a rendering bug: 189 near-duplicate index pages dilute topical authority and spend
crawl budget. Options, combinable: `noindex` on tags with fewer than N posts (the
`page.noindex` hook in `head.html` already exists), expand the alias map, or surface only
tags with ≥3 posts on `/tag/`.

**F7 — `search-text.json` is 2.65 MB raw / 912 KB gzip. RESOLVED IN PART; the original
claim was wrong.** I first wrote that the body index was fetched without user intent.
Tracing the call graph shows it never was: `load()` has exactly one call site,
`requestRender()`, which is itself gated on `hasIntent()`, and `scheduleText()` is
reachable only through `finishLoad()` after that load. An unconditional prefetch would
not merely waste bandwidth, it would throw, because `textCacheKey()` dereferences `DATA`
while `DATA` is still null. So the 912 KB was already unreachable for a visitor who never
searched. See correction C7. What was still worth doing: the invariant was a property of
the call graph three functions away rather than of `scheduleText()` itself, so an explicit
`if (!hasIntent()) return` was added there at zero runtime cost, and the regression test
now asserts that a no-intent visit issues **no** request at all. The size of the payload
remains the underlying issue if the corpus keeps growing.

**F8 — No cache-busting on CSS or JavaScript.**
`/css/main.css` and `/js/*.js` carry no version query while the favicons correctly use
`?v=3`. GitHub Pages serves long `max-age` on these, so returning visitors can run a
mismatched CSS/JS pair after a deploy. The correct pattern is already in the template and
just needs applying consistently.

**F9 — All 83 images are hotlinked, 81 without lazy loading, 3 over plain HTTP.**
There are no local content images (only 5 icon assets). Three `http://` images on
`meldmerge.org` are active mixed content and will be blocked outright on an HTTPS site.
Every page view leaks referrer and IP to third parties (`refactoringguru.cn` ×23, CSDN,
`fishc.com`, …), and the visual layer depends on hosts that can disappear. Self-host into
`assets/`, add `loading="lazy" decoding="async"`, and ship `srcset`.

**F10 — The analytics block contains a trap and a live tracker.**
`google_analytics_id` is empty, but the snippet behind it is the retired UA
`analytics.js`. Anyone who fills that field in gets a silently non-functional property
(GA4 requires `gtag.js` and a `G-` ID). Either migrate to gtag or delete the branch.
Separately, `baidu_tongji_id` *is* set, so a China-specific third-party tracker loads on
every page with no consent mechanism — acceptable for a CN readership, a GDPR problem for
an EU one, and either way it should be a recorded decision. No `security.txt`.

**F11 — The acceptance suite does not test the things that broke.**
All 11 gates pass. None of them asserts: that RSS item descriptions are non-empty (F1);
that `lang`/`og:locale`/`inLanguage` match the article's actual language (F3); that
structured data is not declared twice (F5); that `summary`/`excerpt` is non-empty (`search.json`
currently ships 7 empty summaries); or that new post slugs are ASCII. `audit-content.rb`
reports 31 suspicious classifications and still exits 0. This is the structural reason
F1 survived a fully green pipeline.

### P2

**F12 — Reading-time arithmetic mixes scripts.** `_layouts/post.html` computes
`char_count / 800` and appends a hardcoded `字`. 800 chars/min is right for Chinese but
roughly 4× too slow for the 14 English posts, and the `字` label appears on all of them.

**F13 — The Liquid templates are already quadratic.** `related-posts.html` has 15 `for`
loops, 10 of them nested; `series-nav.html` has 3 nested editorial scans. The same
53-record linear lookup
(`{% for item in site.data.post_editorial.posts %}{% if item.post == X %}…`) is
hand-rolled in 9 places across includes, layouts and the two JSON templates. At 357 posts
that is most of the 76–107 s build. Doubling the corpus roughly quadruples the worst case.
The fix is to precompute a path-keyed index instead of scanning per lookup; it is cheap
now and gets expensive later.

**F14 — The two domains are live and both self-canonical.** Verified: a given article
resolves on `www.cnblogs.com/yongchao/p/<postId>.html` and on the equivalent
`blog.wyclswq.top` path, each emitting a canonical pointing at itself, neither
`noindex`. Same content, two indexable domains, competing canonical signals, and cnblogs
holds the stronger authority for Chinese technical content. This needs a one-time decision
about which domain is authoritative going forward, plus a redirect/canonical plan — and
it cannot be fixed from this repository alone, because Blog Garden's free tier exposes no
per-post canonical tag.

**F15 — Documentation drift.** The parent workspace file
`~/Workspace/Github_yongchaowu/AGENTS.md` §8 states that
`_posts/2026-09-26-mysql-connector-cpp-26.7-call-to-bytes.md` is a deliberately
untracked draft that must never be committed. It is in fact tracked and committed, and the
working tree is clean. Both `AGENTS.md` files are stale on this point.

**F16 — Repository-level polish.** One git tag (`site-v2`) across 15+ substantive commits,
so no changelog and no rollback points. A 16-line README listing 7 of the 9 validators, with
no screenshot, topics list, content policy or contribution note. 48 CSS selectors are
defined in more than one file across `_sass/_v3.scss` and the legacy partials (`.site-footer`,
`.post-card-title`, `.tags-cloud`, `#top`…), so two overlapping CSS systems coexist and
specificity bugs are a latent hazard. Stray files at the repo root (`Todo`, 884 bytes;
`wyclswq.top-master-modification-plan.md`, 12 KB) are excluded from the artifact but
clutter the repository.

## 5. Corrections to earlier passes

Recorded deliberately. A review that only lists its surviving findings is not auditable,
and four of these were wrong in ways that would have caused wasted work.

**C1 — "The sitemap emits raw, unencoded `<loc>` values." Wrong.**
Measured: all 685 sitemap URLs percent-encode non-ASCII (191 post URLs and 48 tag URLs
carry `%`-encoding); `canonical`, `og:url` and internal links are encoded too.
`jekyll-sitemap` handles this correctly. What remains raw is 33 URLs containing `(`,
51 containing `+` and 2 containing `,` — all `sub-delims`, which RFC 3986 permits
unencoded in a path segment. This is an ergonomics and memorability problem, not a
specification violation. **Downgraded from P0 to cosmetic.** The one durable action
remains forward-looking: nothing enforces ASCII slugs for new posts, even though the
author has been writing them by hand since 2026-09.

**C2 — "The 276 tag pages are empty client-rendered shells." Wrong.**
`_site/tag/anaconda/index.html` contains the post title, date, topic path, summary and 13
links inside `<main>`. The grep that suggested otherwise was matching `tag-result`, a class
name that only the client-side filter UI uses. The tag pages are fine; the payload concern
applies only to the `/tag/` index.

**C3 — "The immutability edifice guards the wrong artifact." Wrong, and the most important
error of the three passes.**
`format_fixes.yml` plus the 331-blob baseline *is* the divergence ledger the fork model
needs. Preserving imported blobs so every departure from the cnblogs original is recorded
with a reason is correct design, not a mistake. This was the correct instinct and I read
it backwards.

**C4 — "The llm-probe split/merge is unexplained structural divergence between the two
domains." Wrong.** It is a documented workaround for Blog Garden's 200 KB per-post cap:
`~/Workspace/Blog/AGENTS.md` records that the 284,403 B article reached cnblogs as two
parts while the Jekyll site carries the complete version. Two posts upstream, one here, by
design. Documented in `AGENTS.md` so it is not "fixed" later.

**C5 — "No record of the supply chain exists." Partly wrong.**
`~/Workspace/Blog/AGENTS.md` states the model explicitly: "This workspace is the source of
truth for the articles; that repo is the derived publication target." The chain was
documented — just not visible from inside this repository, which is how I missed it. That
visibility gap is now closed by the new "Content supply chain" section in `AGENTS.md`.

**C6 — "A content-hash diff against the export will detect upstream drift." Wrong, and
demonstrated.** Because the import is deliberately lossy (front-matter adaptation, body-H1
removal, excerpt-separator relocation, fence repairs), comparing normalized bodies reports
**356 of 357** files as changed. Any future drift check must be semantic and per-article,
not mechanical. Matching also has to survive a date-prefix difference (Jekyll filenames are
`YYYY-MM-DD-slug.md`, export filenames are not) and the unstable `.<postId>` suffix that
appears in some snapshots and not others.

**C7 — "A visitor who lands on /search/ and immediately leaves still downloads 912 KB."
Wrong.** See F7. The error was reading `scheduleText()` in isolation and inferring the
eager-fetch behaviour from its own `requestIdleCallback` call, without tracing that its
only caller is gated two levels up. A mutation check settled it: with the intent guard
removed the regression test still passed, which is what a vacuous assertion looks like, and
the honest version of the finding is the invariant rather than a bug that did not exist.

**C8 — Two transcription errors in my own new code, both caught before merge.** While
rewriting `search-interaction-test.js` I dropped the `return` from
`ClassList.contains`, which made the harness silently pass nothing; and in
`generate_tag_pages.py` I first wrote the thin-tag test as
`0 < TAG_NOINDEX_BELOW < post_count`, which noindexed the 56 *thickest* tags and left the
189 singletons indexable — the exact inverse of the intent. Both were found by reading the
rendered output rather than the diff. Recorded because a review that reports only its
successes is not reviewable.

## 7. Overall assessment

The engineering here is well above the norm for a personal blog: eleven acceptance gates,
a blob-bound divergence ledger, a lazily-loaded search index, complete `baseurl` discipline,
and a build that is warning-free. The editorial apparatus — provenance sidecars, visible
AI/import/version-sensitivity states, claim-level citations — is genuinely rare and worth
protecting.

Two structural observations matter more than any individual defect.

**First, the acceptance suite is the largest single risk, not the strongest asset.** All
eleven gates check the repository's *internal* self-consistency: front matter present,
routes generated, blobs unchanged, links resolve. Not one checks the relationship to the
outside world — whether the upstream blog has drifted, whether the same article is
indexable on two domains, or whether private material has entered public history. Those
are exactly where F1, F4 and F14 live. A fully green pipeline is currently evidence that
the self-consistent half of the problem is under control, which is easy to read as more
than it is.

**Second, the correction rate across passes was high** — four substantive claims retracted,
one P0 downgraded, and the one architectural judgment (C3) inverted. Each retraction came
from reading a document or a live page I had not opened in the earlier pass, not from
running more analysis on the same data. The productive next step is therefore not more
static analysis of the repository; it is the cross-workspace and cross-domain checks
(F14, and a semantic drift review against the export) that no current tool performs.

### Second self-review pass — a pre-existing defect, found by accident

Building a `HEAD` worktree as a "before" baseline to prove the `post_editorial` rewrite was
behaviour-preserving, the two builds disagreed on 142 articles' related-post links. The
sets were identical; only the **dates in the URLs** differed, by one day.

The rewrite was innocent. The cause is that `_config.yml` never declared a `timezone`, and
`permalink: /:year/:month/:day/:title/` is derived from each post's front-matter date, which
Jekyll reads in the *build machine's* zone.

| | |
|---|---|
| Posts with a front-matter time between 00:00 and 08:00 | **78 of 357 (22%)** |
| Build in Asia/Shanghai | those 78 URLs move back one day |
| Build in UTC (what CI does) | correct, matches the live sitemap |
| Consequence | a local `jekyll build` / `jekyll serve` produces different URLs than production for a fifth of the archive, so previews silently disagreed, and `AGENTS.md`'s "the permalink is part of the public URL contract" was not actually stable across environments |

Fixed by declaring `timezone: UTC` in `_config.yml`, which matches what CI already forced via
`TZ: UTC` and therefore changes **no** published URL. Declaring `Asia/Shanghai` would have been
more "natural" for a China-based author but would have moved 78 live URLs, so it was rejected.
Verified by rebuilding with `TZ` unset and diffing all 357 article URLs against the live
sitemap: identical. `TZ=UTC` in the documented commands is now belt-and-braces rather than
load-bearing.

`smoke-test.rb` now pins this: `_config.yml` must declare a timezone, and the multiset of dates
in the sitemap must equal the multiset of front-matter dates. That second check was validated
against a deliberately drifted build, where it reports
`front matter has 114 distinct dates, sitemap has 118` — the signature of exactly this bug.

The same pass also produced the equivalence proof for F13, which no amount of reading could
give. Building `HEAD` in an isolated worktree and diffing the rendered output field by field
across all 357 articles:

| Field | Result |
|---|---|
| Article URLs present | 357 vs 357, none one-sided |
| Related-post links | **1428 vs 1428, identical** |
| `series-nav`, source notes, attribution, TOC links | 0 differences |
| Badges | 11 additions, all `reviewed`; 0 removals |

So the O(1) rewrite is behaviour-preserving, and the build went from 76–107 s to 33–46 s.

- **The 188 `noindex`ed tag pages keep their inbound links.** All 188 are still linked from

Two things this pass fixed in my own work:

**A build break I introduced while tidying.** To keep `<!DOCTYPE html>` on its own line I

`Liquid syntax error: 'comment' tag was never closed`. A tag name cannot be written literally
inside a Liquid comment; the note now describes it in words, and the reason is recorded in the

no such key — so the first branch could not fire. Now it reads the sidecar only, and the
comment says where the value comes from instead of implying a second source exists.

## 9. Upstream comparison — 2026-10-01

`scripts/compare_upstream.py` compares the cnblogs export (the source of truth) against
`_posts/`. It exists because the import is deliberately lossy, so a hash diff is useless —
normalising whitespace alone reports 356 of 357 posts as changed. The script instead
normalises the four transformations the importer performs on purpose (front-matter rewrite,
dropped body H1, `<!--more-->` placement, code-fence repair) and then classifies what is
left.

    export articles            346
    repository posts           357
    matched                    331
    identical                   44
    differ, in format_fixes     12   (approved, recorded)
    differ, declaration only    21   (upstream AI/revision notice only)
    differ, spacing only         1
    differ in substance        248   <-- unrecorded

Of the 248, **27 carry version facts that the repository still shows at their old values**,
median 8 changed lines per article, 156 under 10 lines, 13 at 50 or more.

Two representative cases:

- `_posts/2021-11-21-GDB基础.md` still says *"The most recent release (GDB 11.1)"* and
  `gdb -v`. Upstream says, dated 2026-09-25, *"最新正式版本是 GDB 17.2"*, `gdb --version`,
  and https download URLs. The site is six major versions stale and presents them as
  current.
- `_posts/2020-07-08-Tool-Wireshark.md` uses `tcpdump` and the device path
  `\Device\{89515393-…}`. Upstream uses `dumpcap` and `\Device\NPF_{89515393-…}` — the
  `NPF_` prefix is the form Npcap actually requires on Windows, so this is a correction
  rather than a rewording.

**The cause is not drift; it is that the repository was imported before the 2026-09-25
upstream proofread pass and there is no re-sync step.** All eleven original gates check the
repository against itself. None compares it to the source of truth — which is the same blind
spot as F1, F4 and F14, now measured: 248 articles, 27 of them factually behind.

**This is an editorial decision, not a mechanical one.** Most upstream changes are
improvements (version bumps, corrected commands, heading and spacing fixes), but some are
LLM rewrites that change voice and structure substantially — the GDB article's parenthetical
paragraph about package managers was rewritten, not corrected. "Re-import everything" would
import both. It needs a human decision per article, or an explicit policy that the upstream
proofread pass is authoritative and the repository follows it wholesale.

## 10. Upstream sync executed — 2026-10-01

Option C was chosen: sync the whole 2026-09-25 upstream proofread pass, with a review
scheme to follow. The sync itself is done. This section records what it did and, more
importantly, what it refused to do.

### Result

| | before | after |
|---|---:|---:|
| identical to upstream | 44 | **298** |
| differ only by an AI/revision declaration | 22 | **0** |
| differ in substance, unrecorded | 252 | **0** |
| held for review (flow reverses) | — | 19 |

### The direction of content flow is not uniform, and that decided the scope

The whole archive cannot be treated as "cnblogs is the source of truth". Splitting on
2026-06:

- **Before 2026-06 — imported, so cnblogs is the source.** 295 paired posts: 42 already
  identical, 174 carry the upstream proofread mark and this repository did not, 79 differ
  another way, and **none** is ahead of upstream. Syncing is unambiguously forward, so all
  of these were written.
- **From 2026-06 — editing happens here and publishes outward, and the direction reverses
  inconsistently.** 42 paired posts: 17 identical, 14 behind upstream, **1 ahead of it**,
  2 marked on both sides, 8 differing another way.

That 1 ahead is the reason the second group was not written automatically:
`_posts/2026-09-05-New-API-Multi-Process-Docker.md` carries an upstream-absent

> **重要勘误（2026-09-25）**：New-API 是网关/控制面，不是内置模型推理 Worker……

which withdraws the old built-in-worker design. Copying upstream there would have restored
content this site has already corrected. The remaining 24 of the era are reported for review
rather than guessed at, because for them the same mechanical rule would sometimes help and
sometimes revert, and nothing in the text distinguishes the two.

### Four bugs the dry run and the gates caught, each of which would have been silent

1. **An empty upstream body would have overwritten 326 articles with nothing.** `load_parts`
   returned `""` for the no-front-matter case, and 326 of 337 paired exports have no front
   matter at all. A dry run is the only reason this was found rather than written. There is
   now a guard that refuses an empty upstream body when the post is not empty.
2. **Block alignment destroyed every Jekyll-side facility.** Masking `<!--more-->`,
   `{% raw %}` and `{% post_url %}` into atoms before aligning still lost all of them on
   `2020-07-11-Code-OPC-DA`, because the article was rewritten wholesale and
   SequenceMatcher matched nothing to anchor on — while reporting `lost: 0`. Facilities are
   now re-attached by content signature, falling back to fenced-code-block boundaries.
3. **The meldmerge https fix was silently undone.** cnblogs still carries
   `http://meldmerge.org/images/`, so the sync reinstated active mixed content on three
   screenshots and smoke-test.rb failed. `format_fixes.yml` gained a `rewrite` rule that the
   sync re-applies; reverting the file now proves the rule fires.
4. **`search-text.json` decoded entities one layer deep.** A post whose text *mentions*
   `&quot;` renders as `&amp;quot;`, and a pass that ends with `&amp;` -> `&` unwraps only
   that layer, leaving the entity in the corpus. Fixed by ordering `&amp;` first and running
   the list twice; `&#183;` is left alone so a note that discusses an entity still reads
   correctly.

Two upstream text defects were carried in and repaired, both now derived from the text on
every sync rather than re-applied by hand: `label[https://host/p]()` promoted to an autolink (3 in Tool-Git), `[FileZilla Server]()`
left as plain text because cnblogs dropped the target entirely (2 in 书签中的一些工具整理),
and a link target containing a literal space percent-encoded (1 in 吾爱破解-培训第一课).
That last one does not make the link resolve: the URL answers 404 with or without the
encoding, verified 2026-10-01. A dead external link from 2019 is the archive's business.

### Why two identical defects appear twice in format_fixes.yml's history

`书签中的一些工具整理` and `吾爱破解-培训第一课` were fixed on 2026-09-25 and lost again to
this sync, because cnblogs still holds the defective form. Both are now regenerated from the
text on every run, so a third loss is not possible; the entries record both occurrences
rather than the later one overwriting the earlier, because the recurrence is the evidence.

### One definition of "synced", shared

`scripts/upstream_text.py` holds the normaliser, the corrections, and `expected_body()`.
compare_upstream.py and sync_upstream.py both ask it the same question. While each carried
its own copy they answered differently, and the report was the worse of the two: it called
**90** articles drifted immediately after a clean sync, because it did not know about the
`%20` targets, the repaired empty-target links or the re-applied recorded fixes; and **68**
once those were applied, because it still used strict equality where the writer used
typographic equivalence. A report that overstates outstanding work by 3.5x is worse than no
report, so the figure now reads 0 unrecorded and 19 held, and the two scripts cannot
disagree.

### The date threshold was an approximation, and one audit proved it wrong

The rule that kept the 2026-06-and-later era out of the sync was a date comparison. It
happened to place every post with a local correction after that date, but nothing about a
post's content depends on when it was written. An audit of all 253 posts it authorised --
comparing every pre-sync body against its post-sync body and ranking by net lines lost --
found the exception:

`_posts/2024-08-15-Code-C++-regex.md` went from 924 characters to 101. The upstream copy
holds `std::regex;`, `std::smatch;` and `regex_replace();`, and the first of those is not a
valid C++ statement, having no declarator. The repository version is a complete compiling
sample covering `regex_match`, `regex_search`, `regex_replace` and `regex_iterator`. This is
upstream quality falling, not the repository leading, and it would have been reverted
silently.

That audit also cleared the other 252, which is the reason the threshold can be trusted as a
fallback. Content fell by 15% or more in exactly that one post. Ranking by lines lost, the
highest-risk samples were all cases of upstream *correcting* the repository: `SQLite` moved
from 3.11 to 3.32/3.33 and gained a working URL, `Libevent-编译and引用` had its three
inconsistent `2.0.22` residue paths unified to `2.1.11-stable`, and `LD_LIBRARY_PATH` gained
the `DT_RPATH`/`DT_RUNPATH` entries the repository never mentioned along with a fix for an
`export` missing its leading slash. 215 of 253 net gained or held. The largest single gain,
`gcc-源码安装gcc7.5.0` at +773%, is substantive: dependency lower bounds, the fact that
Ubuntu 22.04 and 24.04 ship no installable `gcc-7`, and the
`assertion_failed__xxx is negative` compile-time assertion from building old
`libsanitizer` against a newer glibc.

`upstream_sync: off` now makes the direction a per-post statement rather than a date
comparison, with the evidence in `upstream_sync_reason`. Six posts carry it. The date rule
stays as the fallback for posts that say nothing, and the audit is the reason that fallback
is defensible rather than merely convenient.
